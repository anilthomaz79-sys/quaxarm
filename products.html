Flagship products

Software already in the field, not a slide about what we might build.

Alongside client programmes, Quaxarm designs and operates its own products. Two are live today: Testora for the digital examination ecosystem, and Aegis for Azure cost, security, and readiness.

Digital assessment

Testora

An end-to-end application for the online and centre-based exam process. Exam centres, students, exam providers, and booking operations work in one platform instead of a scatter of spreadsheets, phone calls, and opaque vendor tools.

Ask about Testora

Testora is built for the full stakeholder map: centre managers who must fill seats and stay compliant; candidates who need a fair way to find and book a centre; and exam providers who need auditability, not a black box. Role-based interfaces cover scheduling and bookings, centre selection, operations across multiple sites, and trading of exam-related assets — staff time, rooms, and equipment that would otherwise sit idle.

  • Centre operations: bookings, payments, inventory, and multi-site control
  • Candidate experience: informed centre choice and a clearer booking journey
  • Exam providers: visibility, audit trails, and less reliance on informal process
  • Asset matching: share or trade capacity between centres instead of leaving it unused
  • Transparency: pricing history, compliance support, and operational reporting
Azure estate intelligence

Aegis

Aegis connects to your Azure environment and shows whether the estate is costing more than it should, whether Defender is raising vulnerabilities you have not cleared, and whether resources are actually ready for the load you intend to put on them.

Ask about Aegis

Cloud bills and security consoles are easy to ignore until something fails. Aegis is built for the operating question: can we run this environment safely and economically? It reads the Azure subscription you grant it, then surfaces the three signals leadership and platform teams usually chase in three different portals.

  • Cost optimisation — idle, oversized, or poorly tagged resources that inflate spend
  • Microsoft Defender vulnerabilities — exposure that is known in the tenant but not yet owned
  • Resource readiness — whether compute, network, and configuration are fit for purpose
  • A single view for architecture, security, and finance conversations
